Individual coverage

CHOICE Arrangement

CHOICE Arrangement is the new federal name for an Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement, or ICHRA. Employers can use it to give employees tax-free money for individual health insurance they choose themselves.

CMS and the U.S. Small Business Administration announced the rename in September 2026. The name is new. The 2019 federal rules stay the same.

CMS guide for employers ↗

OpenPlans supplies the plan data

Comparing plans under a CHOICE Arrangement means looking at the individual market. OpenPlans provides that plan catalog and premiums as filed. It also returns provider and drug evidence from public sources.

Missing provider or drug evidence stays unknown. It does not mean "not covered".

Public preview. Self-hosted OpenPlans loads catalogs and filed rates for 30 federal-platform states and 19 state exchanges from the 2026 files, plus separate family-tier imports for New York and Vermont. The hosted preview has 48 states and DC for 2026 and 29 states for 2027. Provider and drug evidence has been measured in three states so far. It is unknown elsewhere.

Agent access.

OpenPlans has a live Model Context Protocol endpoint for the public plan catalog. Agents can search plans, quote them by age and ZIP, and look up provider and drug evidence.

Read-only tools. Stateless Streamable HTTP. Answers include provenance. Provider and drug results may be unknown. Unknown never means "not covered".

Public preview. Coverage is limited. An outside acceptance run is still pending. OpenPlans v0.4.6 is available. The source is on GitHub.

02 / How we work

Principles

  • Public data only. Government agencies and insurers publish the files. We keep the source behind each number.
  • Unknown stays unknown. Missing or blocked data stays unknown. We never fill the gap with a guess.
  • Run it yourself. OpenPlans runs in containers on your server. Its core public data needs no account or key.
  • Checked against the source. Before shipping a release, we compare every value with the raw public files.
  • Respect publishers. Our downloaders identify themselves honestly, obey robots.txt, wait for a review of each publisher's terms, respect access controls and avoid repeat downloads.
